EthoCrypt

Unsupervised behavior mapping of zombie flies. EthoCrypt films one fruit fly from above and below, sorts every moment into behaviors it discovers on its own, checks that each behavior is real, and compares healthy flies with Entomophthora muscae-infected ones. No keypoints, no skeletons, no trained detectors, and no command line for the people running it.

One system

From a fly in the rig to a behavior you can trust

EthoCrypt started as a cluster pipeline. It is now the whole path: hardware to film the fly, software to record it, an app to send it to the cluster, and a report to read the result.

  1. 01 · Film

    Mirror rig

    Two 45° mirrors show one camera the fly from above and below at once, under infrared light, so recordings run through the night.

  2. 02 · Record

    Recorder

    Fixes the camera settings, cuts out and flips each view, saves hourly files with a timestamp per frame, and writes the metadata the pipeline reads.

  3. 03 · Send

    Desktop app

    Signs in to the cluster, uploads the recording, starts the analysis from a lab preset and shows each fly's progress. No terminal, no Slurm.

  4. 04 · Read

    Pipeline & report

    Ten GPU/CPU stages on Harvard's FASRC cluster end in one interactive report: what differs between groups, every cluster's clip, and a verdict on each.

New in 0.3 · The desktop app

Built so an undergraduate can run it on day one

The people who record flies are biologists, often undergraduates with little coding experience. The app hides the cluster behind four screens: Today, Record, Recordings and Experiments.

{% include icon.html name="lock" %}

One sign-in, one connection

A Rust core holds a single connection to the cluster (password and two-factor code, or a key on Harvard networks) for uploads, jobs and progress, so nobody hits the cluster's session limit.

{% include icon.html name="server" %}

Uploads and runs that survive

Uploads resume where they stopped. Adding a fly only processes the new videos, then rebuilds the map and report across every fly. Flies can be pulled in from another experiment without redoing their work.

{% include icon.html name="check" %}

Tracking quality, in plain words

Every fly and view gets good / check / poor, with reasons (frames missed, long gaps, outlines the wrong size, jumps) and pictures of the exact moments flagged.

The problem

Scoring behavior by eye doesn't scale, and it misses what you didn't know to look for

The fungus changes a fly's behavior in ways that are dramatic at the end and subtle before it. To catch the subtle part, you need to measure everything, without deciding in advance what to count.

Typical approach

  • Hand-score a few predefined behaviors
  • Or train a pose tracker on thousands of labeled keypoints
  • Categories fixed before the data are seen
  • Hard to tell a real new behavior from clustering noise

EthoCrypt

  • Segments the whole fly automatically with a foundation model
  • No labels, keypoints or training needed
  • Behaviors emerge from the data and are named afterwards
  • Every cluster is stress-tested for reproducibility before it's reported

How it works

Ten stages, from raw video to a validated behavior map

Each stage is a resumable batch job on Harvard's FASRC Cannon cluster, safe to pre-empt. A preflight check catches missing videos, metadata mismatches or model weights in seconds, before anything is queued.

  1. CPUFind the flyModel the empty arena; the fly is whatever differs from it.
  2. GPUFollow itSAM 2 tracks a full-body outline, legs and wings included, through every frame.
  3. GPULine it upCrop, scale and rotate so every fly is the same size and orientation.
  4. GPUDescribe itDINOv3 features per body region, plus optical flow for limb and wing motion.
  5. CPUCompressPCA, with the variance retained reported every run.
  6. CPUAdd timeWavelet rhythms (Berman) or short frame windows (CASTLE).
  7. CPUMap & clusterUMAP lays every moment on a 2-D map; dense peaks become candidate behaviors.
  8. CPUZoom inThe behavior microscope re-maps one behavior to split it into sub-types.
  9. CPUCheck it's realDoes each cluster survive new random seeds and held-out flies?
  10. CPUCompareTime budgets, bout lengths and transitions: healthy vs infected, per fly.
{% include icon.html name="scan" %}

Keypoint-free

SAM 2 outlines the fly, DINOv3 (or DINOv2) describes each body region, and optical flow measures limb and wing motion relative to the body. Nothing has to be hand-labeled.

{% include icon.html name="chart" %}

Two ways to add time

Berman-style wavelet spectra, as in MotionMapper, capture rhythmic movements like grooming. CASTLE-style short windows capture brief transitions.

{% include icon.html name="target" %}

Behavior microscope

Name a behavior (say, "grooming"), then rebuild the map from only those frames to split it into sub-types and microbehaviors that the whole-repertoire map blurs together.

{% include icon.html name="check" %}

Never over-claim a behavior

Each cluster is tested across random seeds, split halves of the flies, per-fly prevalence and nuisance factors like body size, then marked reproducible, intermediate or unstable.

{% include icon.html name="split" %}

The fly is the unit

A long recording saved as hourly files still counts as one fly: files that share a fly ID are pooled before any test, so ten hours of one fly can't pass for ten flies.

{% include icon.html name="present" %}

A report that leads with the answer

Each map opens on "What differs" between groups, then a review queue sorted by verdict, then the explorable map. Lab members name clusters, or mark "no clear behavior", in the browser.

Hardware

The mirror rig

The fly walks in a flat chamber 1.6 mm deep, between two glass cover slips. A mirror at 45° above it and one below turn both sides toward one camera on the same rail, so a single picture shows the fly from above and from below.

Infrared LED strips light it and an 850 nm filter on the lens blocks visible light, so recordings can run through the flies' dark phase, when the zombie behaviors happen.

  • FLIR Blackfly S
  • 850 nm IR filter
  • IR LED strips
  • 1 × 3 in T-slot rail
  • 3-D printed housing

Beyond flies

Behavioral segmentation, shipped as a product

Strip away the fruit flies and EthoCrypt solves a familiar data-science problem: find natural segments in high-dimensional behavioral data, prove they're real, and put the answer in front of the people who decide.

  • Representation learning with pretrained foundation models instead of hand-built features.
  • Segment validation: stability across seeds and held-out subjects, and checks that no single subject drives a segment.
  • The right unit of analysis: statistics per fly, never pooled frames, the same discipline as per-user rather than per-event metrics in an A/B test.
  • Designed around its users: written product principles, a design system shared by the app, report and docs, and plain-language status everywhere.

Product principles

  1. Anyone in the lab can act on it without machine-vision or command-line training.
  2. Never over-claim a behavior. Uncertain results look uncertain.
  3. Failure is visible and recoverable. Missing inputs and failed jobs surface as plain "needs attention" items, never silent gaps.
  4. Outputs that go into papers stay neutral. Figures and clips serve the science, not the brand.
