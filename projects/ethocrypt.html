EthoCrypt

Unsupervised behavior mapping of zombie flies. EthoCrypt watches one fruit fly per video, sorts every moment into behaviors it discovers on its own, checks that each behavior is real, and compares healthy flies with Entomophthora muscae-infected ones. No keypoints, no skeletons, no trained detectors.

The problem

Scoring behavior by eye doesn't scale, and it misses what you didn't know to look for

The fungus changes a fly's behavior in ways that are dramatic at the end and subtle before it. To catch the subtle part, you need to measure everything, without deciding in advance what to count.

Typical approach

  • Hand-score a few predefined behaviors
  • Or train a pose tracker on thousands of labeled keypoints
  • Categories fixed before the data are seen
  • Hard to tell a real new behavior from clustering noise

EthoCrypt

  • Segments the whole fly automatically with a foundation model
  • No labels, keypoints or training needed
  • Behaviors emerge from the data and are named afterwards
  • Every cluster is stress-tested for reproducibility before it's reported

How it works

Ten stages, from raw video to a validated behavior map

Each stage is a resumable batch job on Harvard's FASRC Cannon cluster. A preflight check catches missing videos, metadata mismatches or model weights in seconds, before anything is queued.

  1. CPUFind the flyModel the empty arena; the fly is whatever differs from it.
  2. GPUFollow itSAM 2 tracks a full-body mask, legs and wings included, through every frame.
  3. GPULine it upCrop, scale and rotate so every fly is the same size and orientation.
  4. GPUDescribe itDINOv2 features per body region, plus optical flow for limb and wing motion.
  5. CPUCompressPCA, with the variance retained reported every run.
  6. CPUAdd timeWavelet rhythms (Berman) or short frame windows (CASTLE).
  7. CPUMap & clusterUMAP lays every moment on a 2-D map; dense peaks become candidate behaviors.
  8. CPUZoom inThe behavior microscope re-maps one behavior to split it into sub-types.
  9. CPUCheck it's realDoes each cluster survive new random seeds and held-out flies?
  10. CPUCompareTime budgets, bout lengths and transitions: healthy vs infected.
{% include icon.html name="scan" %}

Keypoint-free

The fly is segmented by SAM 2, described per body region by DINOv2 (or DINOv3) features, and its limb and wing motion is measured with optical flow relative to the body. Nothing has to be hand-labeled.

{% include icon.html name="chart" %}

Two ways to add time

Berman-style wavelet spectra, as in MotionMapper, capture rhythmic movements like grooming. CASTLE-style short windows capture brief transitions. Pick per experiment.

{% include icon.html name="target" %}

Behavior microscope

Name a behavior (say, "grooming"), then rebuild the map from only those frames to split it into sub-types and microbehaviors that the whole-repertoire map blurs together.

{% include icon.html name="check" %}

Validation built in

Each cluster is tested for stability across random seeds and across maps built from different flies, for whether most flies use it, and for confounds like body size or tracking quality.

Beyond flies

The same problem as behavioral segmentation

Strip away the fruit flies and EthoCrypt solves a familiar data-science problem: find natural segments in high-dimensional behavioral data, then prove they're real before anyone makes a decision on them.

  • Representation learning with pretrained foundation models instead of hand-built features.
  • Segment validation: stability across seeds and held-out subjects, and checks that no single subject drives a segment.
  • Confound checks: is a "behavior" just a proxy for body size or data quality?
  • Production habits: config-driven runs, resumable jobs, preflight validation, and one interactive HTML report per run for non-technical users.
