  • Silencing one circuit reverses a choiceRead as an A/B test in Fig. 2 below

One experiment, read like an A/B test

My Current Biology study asked whether a single brain circuit drives the choice to join a bigger group. Here is the design as a decision-maker would read it.

Question
Does one brain circuit drive the choice to join a larger group?
Unit
The individual animal (male spiny mice)
Treatment
Anterior cingulate → lateral septum circuit silenced with chemogenetics
Control
Same test, circuit not silenced
Primary metric
Preference for the larger vs. the smaller group of peers
Negative control
The same test with groups of novel objects
Result
Preference for the larger group reversed. Objects: no effect.
Source
Fricker et al., Current Biology 2024

The negative control is what makes this useful to a decision-maker: it shows the effect is about social choice, not about "more stuff" in general. It does the same job as a guardrail metric in an A/B test.
