What I bring to a data team

Neuroscience is applied decision science under hard constraints: small samples, noisy signals, expensive experiments and real stakes on getting the causal story right.

{% include icon.html name="split" %}

Experimentation & A/B testing

Randomized, blinded, controlled designs with pre-specified comparisons and adequate power. Define the metric, randomize, remove confounds, then make the call.

In the lab: treatment-vs-control behavioral assays, plus a blinded re-scoring audit I built (BlindTOD) to test a key outcome metric for bias.

{% include icon.html name="target" %}

Causal inference

Correlation is where I start, not where I stop. I test mechanisms by intervening directly, then confirm the effect is specific with matched controls.

In the lab: chemogenetic circuit silencing and CRISPR knockdowns with non-social control conditions.

{% include icon.html name="chart" %}

Behavioral & time-series analytics

Survival (time-to-event) models, rhythms and periodicity, bout and transition analysis: the same math as retention, seasonality and user funnels.

In the lab: circadian activity, periodograms and time-of-death analyses in infected flies.

{% include icon.html name="scan" %}

Machine learning & computer vision

Unsupervised discovery with foundation models (SAM 2, DINOv2), PCA/UMAP and clustering, plus validation so noise is never reported as a new segment.

In the lab: EthoCrypt, a keypoint-free behavior-mapping pipeline.

{% include icon.html name="server" %}

Data pipelines at scale

Config-driven, resumable GPU/CPU batch jobs with preflight validation and automated reports, built to be rerun by someone who isn't me.

In the lab: Slurm pipelines on Harvard's FASRC Cannon cluster.

Featured project

EthoCrypt

Unsupervised behavior mapping of zombie flies. EthoCrypt watches every frame, discovers the behaviors on its own, tests whether each one is reproducible, and compares healthy with infected animals. No keypoints, no hand labels.

Current research

How does a parasite take the wheel?

The fungus Entomophthora muscae infects fruit flies and, just before dusk on the day it kills them, drives a precise sequence: climbing ("summiting"), gluing the mouthparts down, and raising the wings to spread spores.

I study which neural and hormonal changes make that takeover possible, from disrupted circadian clock output to the circuit that triggers summiting.
